# TU-CIS-1051-Final-Project
Final project of CIS 1051 at Temple University


Support Vector Machine models tries to find the hyperplane that best separates two classes by maximizing the margin between the represented by a linear equation. This margin is the distance from the hyperplane to the nearest support vectors on each side.

K Nearest Neighbor models identify the closest data points to an input and makes predictions based on the average value of the data points. KNN assigns categories based on the majority of nearby points, working by proximity and majority to make predictions.

Random Forest models creates decision trees to make predictions. Each tree analyzes random parts of the data and combines them together for regression and predictions. The final prediction is the majority vote of each tree.

Each method was fairly accurate (above 70%), but RF model was most accurate at an average of 87% accuracy. SVM uses linear hyperplanes to make predictions, but this data is not linear, therefore it is not that accurate.

55 PivotTables in Excel in order to determine highly variable relationships between reponses of survey questions. Each survey question was crossed with VOTEGEN_POST, the survey feature this project aims to predict. The PivotTables that showed the highest variability were chosen to use to predict whether someone voted for Donald Trump or Kamala Harris in the 2024 Presidential election. While there was a highly correlated relationship between the various political party affilitation and predicting who a respondant voted for, I decided to leave those out of my prediction model because they are too correlated. This project aims to use other data points in order to make a prediction. The use of this could be very helpful for a political campaign. Simply by knowing these data points, a campaign can use a machine learning model to prioritize certain voters who are predicted in voting one way or another. Typically, political campaigns focus on voters they know will show up to vote and who are on the fence and need convincing. Sending canvassers to talk to voters is a highly effective way of influencing them on the candidates. Political campaigns have limited time and resources, so knowing how someone is likely to vote is incredibly useful information on whether the campaign prioritizes communications with that voter. For example, the Kamala Harris campaign should not prioritize a highly likely Trump voter, becasue that is a waste of time and money. The Harris campaign would be better served communicating with highly likely Democratic voters who they could hire or have volunteer on behalf of the campaign. Lastly, the ever-important middle ground of undecided voters are the top prize for a campaign. If the campaign can influence enough of swing-voters to vote for their candidate, then that could potentially swing the entire election. A machine learning model with a 88% accuracy rating is pretty good enough to make an informed decision on which voters should be prioritized. Predicting past voter behavior is a good indication on future voting behavior. Data mining survey respondants could be useful in future elections. If the survey response is large enough, statistical analysis could be used to create confidence intervals about the population as a whole.
